01

The Seven Roles

Constitutional and functional role of the parliamentarian — remember as a 7-point checklist.

Role 1
Legislative
  • Introducing, debating, voting on Bills — Government and Private Member's Bills
  • Scrutinising delegated legislation and subordinate rules
  • Select/Joint Committees: clause-by-clause examination
Role 2
Representative
  • Voices constituency, regional and social-group concerns in the national forum
  • Link between citizens and the state — grievances, access to schemes
Role 3
Deliberative
  • Debates policy, budgets, public importance — Zero Hour, Short Duration Discussions, Calling Attention Motions
  • “Safety valve” for public opinion and dissent
Role 4
Oversight / Accountability
  • Question Hour, No-Confidence & Adjournment Motions
  • Committee scrutiny: DRSCs, PAC, Estimates, PUC; CAG reports via PAC
Role 5
Financial Control
  • Approves Budget, Demands for Grants, Money Bills — “power of the purse”
  • Ancestry: Commons' “no taxation without representation”
Role 6
Constituency Development
  • MPLADS — ₹5 crore p.a. per MP for local infrastructure
  • Grievance redressal via ministries; privilege to raise local issues
Role 7
Quasi-Judicial / Disciplinary
  • Impeachment of judges/President; privilege matters
  • Disqualification proceedings under the Tenth Schedule

03

Issues in Fulfilling the Role

Eight issues, A to H. Case names and examples are your answer-enrichers.

ADeclining Legislative Productivity & Scrutiny
  • Sitting days: ~127 days/yr in 1950s → under 60–70 now
  • Committee referral collapsed: ~16% in 17th LS vs 60%+ earlier — most laws bypass expert scrutiny
  • Rushed passage: e.g. the three farm laws (2020, repealed) — minimal debate, no committee reference
BDisruptions & Lost Time
  • Disruption as routine opposition tool; sessions at <50% of scheduled time
  • 2016: RS couldn't take up Finance & Appropriation Bills amid protests
  • Question Hour washouts erode the very oversight tools MPs hold
CAnti-Defection Law & the Whip
  • Tenth Schedule (52nd CA, 1985): MPs vote per party diktat → legislators become party instruments
  • Undermines executive accountability, deliberation, and MP-voter accountability (blame the whip)
  • Engineering persists: Karnataka 2019, Maharashtra 2022, TN AIADMK 2026, Telangana BRS→INC 2024–25
DWeak Committee System
  • DRSC attendance ~49–50%; one-year tenure blocks sectoral expertise
  • PAC recommendations non-binding (though ~80% accepted)
  • Thin research/technical staff vs US Congress
ECriminalisation of Politics
  • ADR (2019 LS): ~43% of MPs with criminal cases, ~29% serious
  • Erodes deliberative quality and public trust
FOffice of Profit & Conflict of Interest
  • Ambiguity in Arts 102(1)(a), 191(1)(a)
  • E.g. 20 Delhi MLAs disqualified (2018) — Parliamentary Secretary posts
GConstituency Overload vs National Deliberation
  • Electoral incentives reward visible MPLADS delivery over legislative scrutiny
  • Structural tension: representative vs deliberative functions
HPrivileges: Ambiguity & Misuse
  • Privileges uncodified → uncertainty
  • Sita Soren v. Union of India (2024) overturned P.V. Narasimha Rao (1998): no immunity from prosecution for bribery for a vote/speech

05

Reforms — Four Buckets

Each reform is tagged to its recommending body or case — cite these for marks.

Bucket 1
Structural
  • Mandatory committee referral of all non-urgent Bills (UK practice)
  • Extend DRSC tenure beyond one year (US model)
  • Codify privileges — Privileges Committee, LS (2008)
Bucket 2
Behavioural / Political
  • Confine whips to confidence/money matters; free votes on conscience — 170th Law Commission (1999); NCRWC (2002)
  • Intra-party democracy — II ARC (2007)
  • Time-bound anti-defection adjudication — Keisham Meghachandra Singh v. Speaker (2020)
Bucket 3
Institutional Capacity
  • Expert committee staff — model: US Congressional Research Service
  • Public MP performance dashboards (attendance, questions, PMBs, committee work) — spirit of PRS MP Track
  • Research support per MP — UK House of Commons Library
Bucket 4
Electoral / Accountability
  • Rebalance incentives: reward deliberative performance, not just MPLADS visibility
  • Fast-track criminal cases against sitting legislators (SC directions in PILs)

The parliamentarian is simultaneously a legislator, a representative, a watchdog, and a constituency servant — demanding both national vision and local responsiveness.
