Feature 	Explanation 	Significance
All-India Federation
It proposed a Federation of India consisting of British
Indian provinces and princely states. The federation
was to come into being only after sufficient princely
states acceded which did not happen.
This was the first formal attempt to give
India a federal constitutional structure.
Three-fold division
of powers
Legislative subjects were divided into Federal List,
Provincial List and Concurrent List. Federal law
prevailed in case of repugnancy on concurrent
Became the direct ancestor of the Union,
State and Concurrent Lists in the
Constitution of India.
Provincial autonomy Dyarchy was abolished in provinces and elected
ministries could function in provincial subjects.
It gave us real experience of running
governments before independence,
especially after the 1937 provincial
elections.
Dyarchy at the
Centre
While provincial dyarchy ended, dyarchy was
introduced at the Centre; defence and external affairs
remained under the Governor-General’s direct control.
It showed the contradiction of the Act:
autonomy below, imperial control above.
Bicameralism
A federal legislature with two chambers was
contemplated. Some provinces also had bicameral
legislatures.
It influenced bicameralism in Parliament
and some State legislatures.
Federal Court
The Act established a Federal Court with original
jurisdiction over disputes between the Federation,
provinces and federated states, and appellate
jurisdiction on questions involving interpretation of the
Act.
It became the institutional predecessor of
the Supreme Court of India.
Public Service
Commissions
It provided for a Federal Public Service Commission
and Provincial Public Service Commissions.
This framework influenced Articles 315–
323 of the Constitution.
Expanded franchise The electorate expanded from about 3% to about 14%
of the population.
It widened political participation, though
still far from universal adult suffrage.
Separate electorates
and communal
representation
Separate electorates were continued for Muslims,
Sikhs and others; reserved/communal representation
was also reflected in provincial legislature composition.
It deepened communal political identities
and was criticised by nationalists.
Emergency and
reserve powers
Governors and the Governor-General retained special
responsibilities, discretionary powers and emergency
authority.
Responsible government remained
heavily controlled by colonial officials.
Ordinance power The Governor-General could promulgate ordinances
when the legislature was not in session.
This became an important colonial
antecedent of ordinance-making power in
independent India.
Reserve Bank of
India context
The RBI began operations on 1 April 1935 under the
RBI Act, 1934; the 1935 constitutional framework
recognised modern financial governance needs.
Strengthened centralised monetary and
fiscal administration.

Feature 	Remarks
Historical origin The Assembly was created under the Cabinet Mission Plan, but it became sovereign in
spirit after Independence. It first sat on 9 December 1946.
Representative but indirectly
elected body
It was not elected through universal adult franchise. Members from British Indian
provinces were indirectly elected by provincial legislative assemblies, while princely
states nominated representatives.
Large body 	389-member body responsible for drafting India’s Constitution.
Committee-driven
functioning
Such as the Rules of Procedure Committee, Steering Committee, Finance and Staff
Committee, Credentials Committee, House Committee, Order of Business Committee
and Ad hoc Committee on the National Flag.
Drafting Committee and
Ambedkar’s role
The Drafting Committee scrutinised and revised the draft prepared by Constitutional
Adviser B. N. Rau; Dr. B. R. Ambedkar, as Chairman of the Drafting Committee, spoke
the most in the Assembly.
Intense deliberation Assembly met for 165 days, discussed the constitutional text for 101 days, and
approximately 36 lakh words were spoken during the debates. (PRS)
Stage-wise constitution-
making
The process included a preliminary stage, first reading, second reading and third
reading. The Draft Constitution was published in February 1948, introduced in
November 1948, discussed clause by clause, and enacted on 26 November 1949. (PRS)
Objectives Resolution as
philosophical foundation
Nehru introduced the Objectives Resolution on 13 December 1946; it laid down ideals
of sovereignty, republicanism, democracy, justice and rights, and later influenced the
Preamble.
Public participation
The constitution-making process was not confined to the Assembly alone. The
Constitution of India archive records petitions and feedback from groups, organisations
and individuals to the Assembly.
Dual role after Independence After 15 August 1947, the Assembly also functioned as the Dominion legislature,
handling immediate legislative needs while continuing constitution-making.
Diverse but Eminent
Membership
Eminent lawyers, women, political leaders, social reformers, professors, and former civil
servants, Religious minorities. (Nehru, Ambedkar, Sardar Patel, KM Munshi, Sarojni
Naidu, Hansa Mehta, Frank Anthony, Gumukh Singh Musafir)
Encyclopaedic Borrowing
with Indigenous Vision
UK (Parliamentary govt), US (Fundamental Rights), Ireland (DPSP), Canada (Residuary
Powers, Australia (Joint Sitting), Germany (Emergency Provisions), South Africa (RS
Election)

Challenge 	Nature of the problem 	How the Assembly responded
Partition and
communal violence
The Assembly worked amid displacement,
massacres and insecurity.
It emphasised secular citizenship, minority
safeguards and constitutional order.
Integration of
princely states
India had to unify British Indian provinces
and princely states into one constitutional
framework.
The Assembly designed a Union capable of
political integration and administrative
coherence.
Designing democracy
for a poor and
illiterate society
Many doubted whether universal democracy
could work in India.
The Constitution adopted adult franchise and
parliamentary democracy.
Balancing liberty and
social reform
India needed civil liberties but also caste
reform, land reform and affirmative action.
The Assembly combined Fundamental Rights
with Directive Principles and special provisions
for disadvantaged groups.
Federalism versus
national unity Partition created fear of Balkanisation. The Assembly adopted federalism with a strong
Centre.
Minority protection Communal tensions made minority rights
politically sensitive.
It adopted religious freedom, cultural and
educational rights, and equality provisions.
Language question India’s linguistic diversity made national
language debates deeply contentious.
A compromise was adopted with Hindi, English
and regional languages playing constitutional
roles.
Borrowing versus
originality
The Assembly borrowed from global
constitutions and colonial laws, raising
criticism of imitation.
It adapted borrowed institutions to Indian
needs, creating a distinctive constitutional
synthesis.
Administrative
continuity
Sudden rupture from colonial governance
could create chaos.
The Constitution preserved administrative
continuity while changing the source of
legitimacy from empire to people.
Time pressure The Assembly had to draft amid crisis and
transition.
Despite complexity, it completed the
Constitution in less than three years; Ambedkar
noted that it dealt with 2,473 amendments.
